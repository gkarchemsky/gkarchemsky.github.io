Tags

Every post, filterable by topic.

{%- assign tags = site.tags | sort -%} {%- comment -%} Tags that carry their own colour lead the row, the rest follow in alphabetical order. `ctf` is green everywhere on the site — in this cloud, under every post title, on the home page cards and in the command palette — because a write-up is a different kind of thing from a research post, not just another topic. Sorting it purely alphabetically buried that distinction between two ordinary topics, where the one chip that means something structural read as noise. Written as a list rather than a hardcoded `ctf` so a second accent tag is one word here plus its colour rule in main.css. Kept in step with the `[data-tag="..."]` selectors there — that is the other half of this, and a tag named here without a rule there just sorts first for no visible reason. Partitioned with a loop because Liquid cannot negate `contains`: the negated form of `where_exp` parses and then matches nothing, so the "everything else" half has to be built by hand. Same reason index.html splits the two post streams the way it does. The lists hold tag *names*, not the [name, posts] pairs `site.tags | sort` yields. Pushing those pairs through `push` and `concat` does not survive: the pair comes back out as a Drop rather than an array, `t[0]` then raises "0 is not a symbol nor a string", and the page fails to build at all — with `jekyll serve` quietly continuing to hand out the last good copy, so the edit simply appears to do nothing. Strings survive both filters, and the post list is looked up again below. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign accent_tags = "ctf" | split: "," -%} {%- assign lead_names = "" | split: "" -%} {%- assign rest_names = "" | split: "" -%} {%- for t in tags -%} {%- assign slug = t[0] | slugify -%} {%- if accent_tags contains slug -%} {%- assign lead_names = lead_names | push: t[0] -%} {%- else -%} {%- assign rest_names = rest_names | push: t[0] -%} {%- endif -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- assign ordered_names = lead_names | concat: rest_names -%} {%- if tags.size > 0 -%} {%- comment -%} One list of everything, narrowed in place by the chips above it, rather than a section per tag. The old shape repeated a post once per tag it carried — a post with five tags appeared five times on the page, and finding "the one that was also about heap corruption" meant reading two lists and intersecting them by eye. Selecting tags is the operation people actually want, and it is the one thing a per-tag layout cannot do. Everything is rendered server-side and visible by default, so with no JavaScript this is still a complete, readable archive — it just cannot be filtered. site.js selects `[data-tags]` and hides the rest. {%- endcomment -%}
